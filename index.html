Do heat pumps work in Maryland winters?

Yes — with the right system design. The bigger risk is not “heat pumps don't work in Maryland.” The risk is a unit sized incorrectly for the house, poor ductwork, or an envelope that was never evaluated.

A 1970s split-level in Chevy Chase may need a different approach than a newer townhouse in Germantown. The recommendation should follow the home, not a catalog spec.

When we recommend a heat pump
  • Replacing an aging system with rising operating costs.
  • Switching from oil or propane where electrification incentives materially change the economics.
  • Existing ductwork is functional and appropriately sized.
  • The homeowner plans to stay long enough to capture the value.
  • Utility incentives materially improve project economics.
When we don't
  • Ductwork problems materially change the cost or performance case.
  • Insulation or air-sealing issues should be solved first.
  • Short ownership horizon makes the investment hard to justify.
  • The existing system still has years of reliable life and no compelling upgrade case.
What the installation should include
  • Manual J load calculation.
  • Rebate eligibility review before commitment.
  • Removal of the existing system.
  • Installation, commissioning, and performance testing.
  • Thermostat integration and homeowner walkthrough.
  • Post-install support.
